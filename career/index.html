Nine questions · About 5 minutes · Free · No account

You’ll get an assessment of your whole work mix, a task-by-task explanation, and a next step. “I don’t know” is a useful answer.

Question 1 of 9 · ExposureWhich kinds of work fill your week?

Choose up to three that cover your main responsibilities. Split the time roughly across them; it should add up to 100%.

How much of your week goes to each?

The initial split is even. Adjust it to match your work. These are time estimates, not risk scores.

Question 2 of 9 · ExposureHow repeatable is each kind of work?

Think about the real work, including exceptions. A task with a standard template differs from solving a new problem each time.

Question 3 of 9 · ExposureWhat have AI tools actually shown on these tasks?

Judge the finished result, including corrections. A demonstration or a good first draft is not the same as doing the whole task. For hands-on work, distinguish the physical activity from its paperwork.

Question 4 of 9 · ExposureIs your employer using AI for work like yours?

Your own trials and your employer’s rollout are different observations. Choose what you know about the employer.

Question 5 of 9 · HoldingWhat does the job still need a person to do?

Select all that are essential to the role, even if they take only part of your time. These requirements may shape a future job; they do not guarantee the same staffing.

Question 6 of 9 · HoldingWho owns the final decision or required approval?

Distinguish preparing the work from the authority to decide, approve, or accept responsibility for the outcome.

Question 7 of 9 · HoldingWhat happens when the work is wrong?

Think about the consequences if a mistake gets past normal checks. Serious consequences create a quality requirement, not an automatic promise of job security.

Question 8 of 9 · DirectionWhat does your employer intend to do with time saved?

Choose an announced plan rather than guessing. Saving time can mean more output, different work, or fewer people.

Question 9 of 9 · DirectionWhat is happening to staffing for work like yours?

Think about your workplace over the past 12 months. A staffing change matters, but it is not automatically caused by AI.

    How the assessment works, its limits, and your privacy

    Exposure × Holding, without a false promise.

    Exposure: the assessment looks at task categories, your time estimates, repeatability, and what tools have actually shown. Repeatable digital work has higher potential exposure. Substantial checking or a failed trial is treated differently from a tool completing a task. Physical work needs people or specialized equipment; software that writes instructions does not perform it.

    Holding: physical action, relationships, judgment, approval, and review describe work that may remain. They do not subtract points from exposure or guarantee a job. An approval held by someone else is not counted as your personal protection.

    Direction: employer adoption, plans for time saved, and staffing are considered separately. The main answer assesses your tasks. A separate workplace warning addresses cuts or announced reductions, including when AI is not the stated cause.

    The time shares describe the week you entered. If tasks classified with higher potential exposure make up at least half of it, the result says a large part of your work is exposed. If physical work makes up more than half, the answer focuses on supporting work and equipment automation. These are editorial decision rules, not calibrated employment forecasts. Small differences near a boundary can change the headline; the individual task analysis remains visible.

    There is no trained AI prediction system or live occupation database behind this page. Task descriptions and decision rules are written for this tool. Your choices drive the assessment; no job title or free text is silently treated as if it had been understood.

    Background: the ILO’s May 2025 research on task exposure distinguishes potential changes to tasks from employment outcomes. Its occupational scores are not used here, and it does not validate this questionnaire. The task descriptions are broad starting points; tool performance varies with the task and required standard.

    Privacy: answers stay in this page’s memory. They are not sent to a server or saved in browser storage. Reloading or clearing removes them. Copying or downloading creates a summary you control. The optional newsletter on other pages is separate. Fonts, hosting, and external links may generate normal web requests.
