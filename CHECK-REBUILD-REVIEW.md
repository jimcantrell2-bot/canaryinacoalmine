# Three-step Check rebuild — October 8, 2026

The live check was reviewed on October 8, 2026. Its strongest material is the distinction between tasks, human involvement, observed tool use, and staffing changes. The original bird illustrations and restrained cream, yellow, and dark green palette are retained.

## Substantive changes

- The prior nine-question check and earlier six-screen prototype ask several overlapping questions about review, responsibility, licenses, judgment, and error consequences. Those distinctions can be useful, but the result was too broad for the available evidence. The new version examines one recurring task in three steps.
- Job category alone does not establish the effect of AI. The check asks what the user has actually observed for a named task rather than classifying a whole job by broad task groups.
- Human involvement is recorded as work to investigate, not protection from cuts. It never cancels a staffing warning. “Holding” and “exposure” are replaced by plain language.
- Cuts, vacancies, and mixed staffing patterns get different actions. Explanations linking reductions to AI remain reported explanations rather than proof of causation. Unknown answers remain visible.
- The output gives one next step and one workplace question, followed by its reasons. Users can edit, copy, save as text, and start another task.
- The overview panel is a straight, responsive part of the layout with no rotation, offset positioning, or decorative asymmetry. Following Jim's screenshot feedback, its background is cream and yellow is a border accent, keeping the yellow bird visible. The interaction uses visible radio buttons, checkboxes, progress, errors, keyboard focus, and a back control.
- Answers are held in page memory only. They are cleared on reload or reset and are not submitted to a server. Newsletter signup and unrelated promotional sections are omitted from the check.

AVP's current get-help page lists limited weekday hotline hours; it is not labelled 24/7. New entries retain source links and individual review dates. The rest of the directory was not re-reviewed in this update.

The ILO's May 2025 task exposure study supports distinguishing potential task changes from job replacement. It does not validate this questionnaire or its rules. No probability, occupation ranking, or prediction of job loss is provided. This compact tool deliberately covers one task at a time, using the user's observations rather than independently measuring tool capability or the labor market.
